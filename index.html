Register switch: same future, different voice

Study13 minPairs in breakout rooms

Grammar focus: register in future reference

Informal (chat, friends, colleagues)

  • going to / spoken gonna (never in writing)
  • present continuous: We're sending it tomorrow.
  • be about to: I'm about to leave.
  • I'll get back to you.

Formal (business, official, academic)

  • will + passive: The contract will be sent tomorrow.
  • be to (official plans): The minister is to visit the region.
  • be due to (scheduled): The report is due to be published in May.
  • future continuous (polite, neutral): We will be contacting you shortly.
  • look forward to + -ing; Would you like me to…? for formal offers

Day one: the new employee's questions

Study15 minSolo, then poll

Modal reference card

1 · Certainty (deduction)

NowPast
≈ 95% yesmust bemust have been
≈ 50%may / might / could bemay / might / could have been
≈ 95% nocan't / couldn't becan't / couldn't have been

Negative deduction = can't, never mustn't.

2 · Obligation & advice

  • must: the speaker's rule / strong feeling; have to: external rule. Past of both: had to.
  • mustn't = it's forbidden. don't have to / don't need to / needn't = it's not necessary.
  • should / ought to = advice. need to = necessity.
  • In American English, must not and don't need to / didn't need to are more common in speech than mustn't and needn't.

Form: modal + bare infinitive: must to go, can goes. Only have to, need to, ought to take to.

3 · Modal perfect (modal + have + past participle)

  • must / might / can't have done → speculating about the past
  • should / shouldn't have done → criticism or regret
  • could have done → a possibility that didn't happen
  • needn't have done → you did it, but it wasn't necessary
